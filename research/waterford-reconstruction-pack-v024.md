# Waterford / Veðrafjǫrðr Archaeological Reconstruction Pack v0.24

## Scope

Public-safe research pack for the Early Medieval Ireland Explorer, focused on Waterford c. AD 900–1050.

This pack is deliberately conservative. It separates:

1. directly observed excavation evidence;
2. published historical interpretation;
3. constrained spatial relationships;
4. Geometry D reconstruction choices.

No excavation plan or source-style traced geometry is reproduced here.

---

## 1. Historical frame

### AD 900

Waterford has a pre-914 Viking occupation / longphort context in historical summaries, but the exact footprint of that first settlement remains unresolved. The public reconstruction should therefore show **presence without false urban precision**.

### AD 950

The original 10th-century Hiberno-Norse settlement is interpreted in published excavation discussion as centred on the eastern end of the triangular promontory, around Dundory / the later Reginald's Tower area. This is a **source-backed interpretation**, not an excavated AD950 town boundary.

### AD 1000

Westward development is plausible and consistent with later Viking urban evidence, but an exact AD1000 street grid, defence line and quay frontage cannot currently be reconstructed at Geometry B.

### AD 1050

The 11th-century urban fabric is materially better constrained. Arundel Square / Peter Street produced late Viking houses, an 11th-century paved street surface, three-aisled houses with clay floors and wattle walls, smaller rear structures, paths and property boundaries. This supports a more structured urban language, but not a complete mapped AD1050 city.

## 2. Direct archaeological controls

### Bailey's New Street — 1999 excavation

Public coordinate:

- ITM E 660987 / N 612422
- 52.260127, -7.106629

Direct observations:

- two large roughly north–south ditches;
- F207 approximately 5.7 m wide and 1.3 m deep;
- ditch fills contained late 12th/early 13th-century material;
- one ringed pin may date to around AD950.

Interpretation boundary:

The report states that the ditches are **likely** related to defences east of the excavation and **may** mark a western limit of the area later known as Dundory. The project therefore treats:

- ditch existence = direct excavation evidence;
- Dundory-western-limit interpretation = Evidence B / Geometry C;
- exact AD950 defensive line = unresolved / Geometry D only if rendered.

### Arundel Square — 1990 excavation

Public coordinate:

- ITM E 660737 / N 612452
- 52.260424, -7.110284

Direct observations include:

- late Viking houses;
- full ground plans of several 11th/12th-century houses;
- earliest Peter Street surface associated with 11th-century structures;
- three-aisled houses with clay floors and wattle walls;
- smaller houses behind street-fronting buildings;
- paths and property boundaries;
- pits including wood-lined examples.

This is currently the strongest public-safe control for **11th-century Waterford urban morphology**.

### Peter Street / Olaf Street — 1988 excavation

Public coordinate used by the excavation record:

- ITM E 660637 / N 612352
- 52.259537, -7.111766

Direct observations include:

- multiple timber houses along Peter Street;
- trampled clay floors, central hearths and wattle/stave walls;
- extensive backlands;
- sunken-featured structures;
- a door-jamb timber dated to approximately AD1083 (reported uncertainty in source).

Chronological rule:

The AD1083 timber is a **post-1050 control**. It may inform late-11th-century development but must not be silently projected into AD1050.

### Bakehouse Lane — 1988 excavation

The excavation record reports:

- approximately 12 m of Viking town defences excavated;
- ditch up to c. 3 m deep and 8 m wide;
- inner bank up to c. 6 m wide;
- stone revetment;
- dendrochronological determinations from ditch timbers centred on AD1070–1080.

Chronological rule:

**AD1070–1080 is not AD1050.**

This feature is a strong negative control against back-projecting the known late-11th-century defensive line into the AD1050 phase.

### High Street

Published excavation reporting describes High Street as one of the principal late Viking streets. This supports a late Viking street-axis claim, but it does not provide an exact AD950/1000 street centreline for public mapping.

### Henrietta Street

Public coordinate:

- ITM E 660890 / N 612464
- 52.260510, -7.108030

Testing encountered probable undisturbed organic archaeological strata around the Peter Street / St Olaf's Church area. It is useful as an archaeological survival control, not as a mapped Viking building footprint.

## 3. Hydrology and topography

### River Suir

The River Suir is the primary northern / north-eastern maritime edge of the Hiberno-Norse town.

### St John's River / marsh

The 1999 Bailey's New Street report describes Hiberno-Norse Waterford as occupying triangular ground bounded by:

- River Suir to the north-east;
- marshy ground around St John's River to the south-east;
- the apex at the later Reginald's Tower area.

This supports a **Geometry C historical topographic relationship**.

It does **not** establish a precise AD950 marsh polygon or shoreline.

### Early quayside

The National Monuments Service urban archaeology survey states that the original quays were probably established in the Viking period, but that virtually nothing was known about the form, date or size of the first quayside or its successors.

This uncertainty must be preserved as data:

- river-facing activity MAY be shown;
- timber landing / beaching MAY be shown schematically if clearly interpretive;
- a continuous measured quay MUST NOT be shown;
- a later monumental stone quay MUST NOT be shown.

## 4. Reconstruction constraints

### AD 900

#### MUST SHOW

- River Suir maritime setting;
- early Viking occupation / longphort context;
- low spatial certainty.

#### MAY SHOW

- sparse timber activity;
- boats / landing activity in generalized form;
- light enclosure language only if marked interpretive.

#### MUST NOT SHOW

- later Viking Triangle street grid as fixed;
- stone Reginald's Tower;
- mature western city;
- measured quay frontage;
- Bakehouse Lane late-11th-century defence.

### AD 950

#### MUST SHOW

- River Suir;
- St John's River / marsh relationship;
- eastern Dundory-focused core;
- earthwork/ditch defence as plausible but unresolved.

#### MAY SHOW

- generalized timber houses;
- craft/service structures;
- modest river-facing activity;
- schematic ditch/bank language on the landward side.

#### MUST NOT SHOW

- Bailey's New Street ditches as a proven exact AD950 circuit;
- continuous stone wall;
- modern Reginald's Tower form;
- mature western late Viking city;
- monumental or measured Viking quay.

### AD 1000

#### MUST SHOW

- east-to-west growth relative to AD950;
- Suir / St John's hydrological frame;
- timber urban fabric.

#### MAY SHOW

- developing street axes;
- yards, paths, craft/storage structures;
- more continuous occupation toward later Peter / High Street areas.

#### MUST NOT SHOW

- exact Bakehouse Lane AD1070–1080 defensive geometry;
- post-Conquest stone city circuit;
- precise early quay line;
- fully mature 12th-century frontage.

### AD 1050

#### MUST SHOW

- denser timber urban fabric;
- street-fronting houses plus rear structures;
- paths / property boundaries;
- pits and storage/backland activity;
- westward expansion relative to AD950.

#### MAY SHOW

- three-aisled timber houses;
- clay floors / wattle-wall visual language;
- generalized High Street / Peter Street axes;
- heterogeneous rear-yard activity.

#### MUST NOT SHOW

- Bakehouse Lane AD1070–1080 defensive ditch as the default AD1050 boundary;
- Peter/Olaf AD1083 feature as if securely present in AD1050;
- Anglo-Norman stone circuit;
- 13th-century quay frontage;
- modern Reginald's Tower architecture.

---

## 5. Geometry contract

### Geometry B

Allowed for independently reproducible excavation-site coordinates.

A Geometry B site point does **not** upgrade every feature excavated within the site to Geometry B in public mapping.

### Geometry C

Used for:

- Dundory eastern-core interpretation;
- River Suir / St John's marsh spatial relationship;
- westward development trend;
- likely defensive relationship of Bailey's New Street ditches;
- late Viking street-axis relationships where source plans are not reproduced.

### Geometry D

Used for:

- phase occupation envelopes;
- rendered defensive corridors;
- generalized street axes;
- reconstructed shore / marsh appearance;
- individual houses in future bird's-eye imagery.

---

## 6. Rights / provenance

Public site may use:

- source titles and URLs;
- public excavation-site coordinates;
- short factual summaries;
- project-created schematic geometry.

Public site must not reproduce:

- excavation plans without separate rights clearance;
- traced source-plan geometry;
- copyrighted figures from published excavation volumes;
- source imagery merely because it is technically accessible.

---

## 7. Bird's-eye generation gate

Waterford is **not yet approved for final bird's-eye generation**.

Before image generation, the following still require a dedicated review:

1. phase choice: AD1000 vs AD1050;
2. historical shoreline / marsh visual envelope;
3. street-axis confidence;
4. exact degree of westward occupation;
5. defensive-corridor treatment;
6. waterfront treatment;
7. whether St Olaf's ecclesiastical context is included for the chosen year.

The current preferred candidate for first visual reconstruction is **AD1050**, because the 11th-century urban evidence is materially stronger than the 10th-century spatial evidence, while the AD1070–1080 defence remains explicitly excluded.
